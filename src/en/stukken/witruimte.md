A good page is half made of nothing. The margins, the space between the lines, the empty spot above a heading: it is all design, even if you don't see it.

Three things make the difference between a text you read and a text you skim:

1. **Line length.** Between forty-five and seventy-five characters per line reads most comfortably. Any longer, and the eye loses the thread on the way back.
2. **Line spacing.** Slightly more generous than the default: one and a half times the type size is a good start.
3. **The typeface itself.** A typeface with a calm rhythm, which works equally well on screen and on paper.

---

It is tempting to fill a page. There is always another block, a button, a recommendation that could go on it. But every addition asks for attention, and attention is exactly what the reader wants to give to the text.

### A test

Read this paragraph and notice how often your eye jumps back. If the line is too long, you search for the start of the next line; if it is too short, the rhythm stutters. On this site, the measure is set at about sixty-five characters.
