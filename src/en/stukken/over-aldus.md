Around 1500, Aldus Manutius printed books in Venice that fitted in a coat pocket. That sounds small, but it was a revolution: until then, books were heavy folios that lay on a lectern. With Aldus, reading became something you could do anywhere — on the road, in bed, alone.

## Dolphin and anchor

His printer's mark was a dolphin wound around an anchor, with the motto *festina lente*: make haste slowly.[^1] It is a motto that suits a site that brings no news, only pieces that take their time.

> Festina lente — make haste slowly.

## What you will find here

This site collects pieces about language, about what people say and what they mean by it. No timeline, no comments, no notifications. Only text, and enough white space to let that text breathe.

[^1]: According to Suetonius, the motto goes back to the emperor Augustus. Erasmus wrote an extensive reflection on it in his *Adagia*, which Aldus printed in 1508.
